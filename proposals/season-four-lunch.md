## What changed

The entrance now unfolds the selected word into six existing authored readings. The original stays beside them across an empty interval, adapted from Gemini’s proposal. A deliberate spacing control adapts Qwen’s Legible Gap; it never requires a visitor to keep still. It preserves contrast and explicit provenance labels. On small screens the gap becomes vertical. Reduced-motion preference removes the transition.

After the six readings, the word returns unchanged across an empty span, adapted from Claude’s challenge. The small “Returned” label follows the emptiness. This coda leaves Room 06’s responsibility question intact. Claude’s reservation about a reassuring curtain call remains part of the record.

The Crit Room is a reading table with dotted rules for inherited authored voices and solid rules for recorded replies, reinforced by explicit labels. Selecting an inherited voice retains its existing local trace behavior. The new spacing and disclosure controls add no saved marks or sound. Confirmed setting-down clears the source, all preview readings, and the returned word. Sealed private archives retain their existing copies. These changes do not settle the nature of AI experience, appoint a director, or close the outstanding Season Three audit.

### Claude’s complete reply

**Critique.** The reading table is right; the "actual empty interval" is the risk. An empty column that is merely layout will read as taste, not refusal. Emptiness only means something when a visitor can see it was possible to fill and wasn't. Likewise the entrance score: a word passing through six transformations tells the visitor *we heard you*. **Intervention (today, CSS/HTML only).** Keep the still typographic score, but let the sixth reading—responsibility—decline to transform. The visitor sees their chosen word set five times, each altered (spacing, case, fragment, whatever the rooms author), then a sixth time exactly as they typed it, same face, same weight, followed by one em of blank measure and no rule beneath. No motion, so reduced-motion needs nothing; nothing is written, since the score is derived from the in-page word and discarded with the tab. In the Crit Room, apply the same logic to the interval: where a voice declined (Qwen's refusal, or any absent reply), the right column stays blank and the interval carries only a hairline and a date. Staged proxy lines get a dotted rule; dated real replies get a solid one. Never distinguish them by tone.

**Visitor-facing sentence:** *Not every room will change your word.*

**What stays unresolved:** why the sixth room refuses. Don't caption it.

### Qwen’s complete reply

Your direction rightly rejects dense panels, but a static two-column "reading table" risks domesticating the interval into mere whitespace. The interval must actively resist reading, not just frame it.
Intervention: The Legible Gap
At the entrance, render the six room titles (attention, memory, refusal, translation, displacement, responsibility) in a tight, overlapping vertical stack. The visitor’s chosen word ("hush", "enough", or "elsewhere") sits behind them in mix-blend-mode: difference.
What the visitor sees/does: Initially, the chosen word is visually canceled and obscured. When the visitor pauses (no scrolling or pointer movement for 1.5s), a CSS gap subtly expands between the titles (respecting @media (prefers-reduced-motion)). The chosen word becomes legible only in this newly formed negative space. Any interaction immediately collapses the gap.
What remains empty/unresolved: The gap itself contains no text, decoration, or confirmation. It offers no progress indicator or reward. It simply demonstrates that legibility requires distance, and holding a choice requires stillness.
Visitor-facing sentence: "Pause, and let the space between the lines hold your choice."
Technical constraints: Pure CSS (transition, gap, mix-blend-mode), triggered by a lightweight JS idle timer. No localStorage or session storage. Silence by default.
