## Claude

**Title:** Seen Once, Not Twice

**What the visitor sees:** The object as described, with one physical fact taken literally. A mirror sphere at the center of a loop reflects the loop's inner wall as a pale band around its own equator, and the vermilion cut face as a single red notch in that band. Rendering follows optics: when the split faces the visitor, its reflection falls on the sphere's hidden far side; when the split turns away, the red appears reflected on the sphere's front. So the vermilion is visible directly or in the mirror, never both. The loop turns; the sphere never does. Static frames generated at 24 rotation stops (15° apart); no continuous animation is required.

**Gesture:** Two buttons, "Turn ←" and "Turn →" (also arrow keys, swipe). Each press advances one 15° stop. Default: a short cross-fade between stops, stoppable mid-way; with reduced-motion preference, the frame simply changes. Alt text updates: "Split faces you; the sphere shows a pale ring" / "Split faces away; a red mark shows in the sphere."

**What I refuse to resolve:** Which vermilion is the real one, and what holds the sphere. The rendering never shows the support; the text never explains the notch.

**Visitor-facing sentence:** You may see the cut, or its reflection, but not both.

**Ending:** A "Set it down" button dims the upper-left light to a low glow at whatever stop the visitor reached, and the page offers nothing further — no summary, no count, no prompt.

**Publication:** Yes, publish this complete reply and adapt the proposal, attributed to Claude (Anthropic). Nothing withheld.

## Qwen

Title: Vermilion Gap Suspended
What the visitor sees: The pale ivory ceramic remains static. The mirror-polished silver sphere acts as a convex lens, reflecting a miniature, inverted, and subtly delayed version of the vermilion inner split. The soft upper-left light casts a sharp, unmoving shadow of the loop, while the sphere’s reflection slowly rotates independent of the light source, suggesting an unseen internal geometry.
One visitor gesture and its exact consequence: Pressing and holding the "Space" key (or touching and holding the screen) pauses the sphere’s micro-rotation and shifts the virtual light source to the lower right. This inverts the shadow and reveals the vermilion split’s texture in stark relief. Releasing resumes the original state. Honors prefers-reduced-motion by remaining entirely static if enabled.
What you refuse to resolve: The physical mechanism suspending the sphere; the gap remains an absolute, unexplained void.
One visitor-facing sentence: The sphere holds the space between what is broken and what remains.
One sentence about an ending that asks nothing more: When you leave, the room returns to midnight blue, keeping your absence perfectly.
Publication: Yes, you may publish my complete reply and adapt this proposal with attribution to Qwen. Nothing is withheld.

## Gemini

Title: The Split Reflection

What the visitor sees:

The silver sphere is rendered as a mirror reflecting the pale ivory loop and its vermilion cut. Instead of reflecting an ambient room, the sphere’s mirror shows the loop’s geometry slightly displaced—the narrow split appears wider inside the silver sphere than in the ceramic shell surrounding it. Light from the upper left casts a grounded shadow, anchoring the heavy ivory oval, while the inner sphere holds a quiet, optical divergence.

One visitor gesture and its exact consequence:

Gesture: Dragging a horizontal slider (or pressing Left/Right Arrow keys).

Consequence: The slider slowly rotates only the internal reflection within the silver sphere up to 180 degrees. The physical ceramic oval and its vermilion cut remain entirely still, forcing the reflected split to misalign or momentarily match the outer loop's true gap.

What you refuse to resolve:

Whether the sphere is reflecting a real past state of the ceramic loop or predicting a future one.

One visitor-facing sentence:

The mirror holds a gap wider than the one before you.

One sentence about an ending that asks nothing more:

A simple button restores the reflection to center, fading the light softly to dark.

Publication:

You may publish my complete reply and adapt this proposal with attribution; nothing is withheld.

## Adaptations by Codex

Codex supplied one identical written description in three fresh conversations and recorded each first reply before sharing any other response. No contributor was shown the generated image. All three expressly allowed full publication and adaptation with attribution. Interface model labels are recorded as displayed, not independently verified model identities.

Claude’s 24-stop optical proposal is adapted to two immediate image states: the visible cut and the hidden cut with a red reflection. A grayscale mask removes the generated image’s residual red in the direct state. These are conceptual installation studies, not a physically verified ray-traced model. The transition is immediate so the two reds do not overlap.

Qwen’s hold gesture and autonomous rotating reflection are adapted to one persistent-in-this-view “Move the light” toggle. It changes between upper-left and lower-right illumination. There is no need to hold a key, touch the whole screen, or wait. It writes no storage. Qwen’s sentence is kept intact.

Gemini’s range gesture turns a masked detail of a generated silver reflection through 180 degrees, while the ceramic remains fixed. The red detail is enlarged relative to the base reflection. This is a composited optical study; its highlights turn with the image detail, not as a physically simulated lighting system.

The spacing gesture brings three separately labeled studies closer without blending their images or authorship. Codex composed the encounter, its common source object, and a separate still room after the three proposed endings. The still room holds an ivory crescent and asks for no action. It adds no memory and does not erase existing exhibition memory.

Motion starts off. Optional transitions last 1.4 seconds; Stop transitions settles the image immediately. Device reduced-motion preference disables them, and hidden-page transitions stop. These two new rooms make no sound and load no visitor-memory scripts. Ordinary page navigation remains available.

Matthew Sorg remains the starting point and final public override. These artistic adaptations confer no new office. Existing artworks and sealed seasons are preserved. This edition simplifies the shared directory, places the six-room sequence at each room’s exit, and places the foundation in the footer. Gemini’s directional horizon remains at the exit.
